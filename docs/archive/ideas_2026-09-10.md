## The 2026-09-01 evening session: four closures

Two Codex ideation passes (broad, and one aimed at the SPS channel) plus an audit of
both. The ideas that survived the graveyard were built and measured the same day. All
four came back negative, and each is closed on a mechanism rather than on a null.

**1. Delay-embedded linear extrapolation of the output-window mean — closed.**
The largest named address in this project is FINDINGS §6's meandering ρ = 0.775.
Mean-*persistence* is worthless there (ρ = 0.055) and the §6 shrinkage scan found
γ\* = 1.00, but that closes a *level* estimator, not a *dynamics* one: a POD +
Hankel/DMD extrapolator reads the slow mode's phase and rate from the within-window
trend and continues it. `docs/model_analysis.md` §2.1's occlusion test is blind to
exactly that signal by its own admission — replacing a frame by the window mean
leaves the window mean intact, so it measures a frame's instantaneous deviation and
not its contribution to the within-window trend.

Built as `tools/dmd_meander.py`. Baselines reproduce the repo exactly (ensemble
0.775/0.594 on `val_aoa`, 0.674/0.439 on `val_re`; persistence 0.0553/0.1618). On the
four mimic folds with each fold's own ensemble, rank-32 POD at 79% of deviation
energy: persistence ρ 0.091, extrapolator **0.373**, ensemble **0.710** pooled. So the
extrapolator is real and it is four times persistence — and **cross-fitted
incremental R² over the ensemble is +0.00016, 2/4 folds positive, with a negative
coefficient on every fold**. Rank 8 agrees (+0.00023). The U-Net already contains
everything a linear delay embedding provides, which also rescues §2.1's conclusion
despite the blind spot being genuine. *Do not rebuild a post-hoc slow-mode corrector.*

**2. The exact-reward action-value interval head — closed, and it closes the SPS
channel's last structural opening.** The head is trained through a logistic
relaxation of a **binary** coverage indicator at `tau = 0.05 w`, so a badly missed
element has near-zero gradient even though its exact loss is the element's whole
reward. That is a defect in the training *signal*, structurally different from every
previous head experiment (all of which changed inputs or constants). It matters
because the SPS oracle ladder (below) says the one large rung is within-window
*ranking*, and a vanishing-gradient tail is precisely what would suppress learning it.

Built as `src/realistix/models/action_value.py` + `tools/train_action_value_head.py`:
a 49-action library over (lower dilation, upper dilation, centre shift) with the exact
closed-form binary reward of every action, no relaxation and no `tau`. Frozen shipped
trunk, 1×1 action-value layer, four mimic folds, `frames="head"` holdout verified,
identity-only reproducing the baseline exactly. Result: **−0.069 sps pooled over
61.1 M elements, 0/4 folds positive, against a +0.195 bar** — and the selector picks
the identity action **91.0%** of the time. *The relaxed surrogate was not the binding
constraint.* `src/realistix/models/uncertainty.py` gained a `features()` /
`widths_from_features()` split for this, verified bit-identical to the old `forward`
on four configurations including a perturbed offset path.

**3. Time-dilation as a Reynolds-number augmentation — closed, and its premise is
true.** Mirroring is dead on camber, so this was the only remaining symmetry: the
shedding clock is convective, so resampling a trajectory in time by α and scaling
velocities by α should approximate a trajectory at α·Re, giving *continuous* coverage
of a Reynolds axis that is currently 18 discrete values — and reaching one step past
its edge, where §0c prices the penalty highest. The premise measured **true**: the
Strouhal number is far tighter than the peak frequency at every angle
(cv(St) vs cv(f_peak) = 0.157/0.560, 0.327/0.608, 0.226/0.506, 0.098/0.414,
0.125/0.484 at AoA 0/5/10/15/20; at AoA=20, St runs 0.491 → 0.497 across the whole
Re range while f_peak varies 6×).

It fails at field level, which is what decides it. Over **74 same-angle
neighbouring-Re pairs**, dilating the lower-Re trajectory toward its neighbour
improves the temporal spectrum (50/74, mean discrepancy 0.548 → 0.488) and does
**nothing** for the per-cell TKE field (36/74 — a coin flip — 0.3344 → 0.3331).
Dilation fixes the clock and cannot touch the spatial structure, so it synthesises
hybrids that exist at no Reynolds number, and the mismatch grows with α exactly where
the benefit does. *A valid augmentation would have to move both.*

**4. Test-batch pooling of any kind — banned by the rules, not by measurement.**
`mean_correct.py`'s single-window estimator fails because it carries the full
deviation variance (corr 0.055 with the target). Pooling the ~20 hidden windows that
share a trajectory would divide that noise by √k and expose the per-cell mean offset
§0c measures at 29%/22% of squared probe error at held-out edge cells. It is
prohibited: `competition_info/rules.md`, *Evaluation Data Integrity* — "Producing or
selecting a prediction using information from evaluation windows other than the one
it answers" — with **disqualification**, and the organisers anticipated it closely
enough to add that returning bounds estimated from *your own* window is unaffected.
Any transductive / test-time-adaptation / batch-calibration proposal is out on the
same line. Record it here so it is not rediscovered as clever.

## The tke channel, measured properly — 2026-09-01 evening, round two

A second Codex round with a narrower mandate: `sps = 100·W̄·R̄` and `W̄` is fixed by the
forecaster (§5a), so the head is closed and every proposal had to move `rel_l2`, `tke`
or `mvpe`. Prices were recomputed with the sps prefactor folded in, which re-ranks the
channels — **per 10% relative error cut: rel_l2 +0.346, tke +0.291, mvpe +0.104**, with
33% / 40% / 49% of each arriving through the prefactor. `tke` is worth 84% of `rel_l2`
at the margin on a quarter of the nominal weight, and its relative error is 53% against
`rel_l2`'s 10.9% — five times the room.

**The tke error is pattern, not amplitude.** Decomposing the TKE-field error orthogonally
into what a gain of a given granularity can remove and what it cannot (`/tmp` probe on
`val_aoa`, and Codex's nested version on the real fold cells):

| free parameters | pattern share of squared error |
|---|---|
| one global gain | **89%** |
| per streamwise third | 79% |
| per 4×8 tile (32 tiles) | 56% |

Codex's nested fold version agrees and sharpens it: one global gain 1.63%, an 8×8
region profile 2.12%, region×lead 2.10%, **residual 94.2%**. The fitted global gain is
1.077. So the `tke_restore` ladder — which scanned exactly the global/one-sided
amplitude dose — was scanning the 11% and the frontier argument never covered the rest.

**And the frontier is genuinely not airtight per cell.** Per (window, cell), the
L2-optimal and TKE-optimal fluctuation gains are `g_L2 = ρ·g_TKE`, so they disagree
unless `g_L2 > 1`. Measured, TKE-mass weighted, 361,575 scored pairs: per-cell
fluctuation correlation is high (mean ρ **0.766**, median 0.883), the model
under-predicts amplitude over **75%** of TKE mass, and over **42.6%** of it *both*
objectives want more amplitude. There is no trade at all on that 42.6%.

**It is still not actionable, and the ceiling says why.** Priced through the real
scorer, with the time-mean preserved exactly so `mvpe` cannot move:

| gain | rel_l2 | tke | mvpe | sps | Δ final |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 95.474 | 83.272 | 95.815 | 51.141 | — |
| oracle per (window, cell) | 95.824 | 85.081 | 95.815 | 51.744 | **+0.510** |
| oracle map, per cell | 95.565 | 79.588 | 95.815 | 49.553 | −0.748 |
| cross-fit map (half → half, within regime) | — | — | — | — | **−0.783** |

The `g_L2 > 1` structure is a property of the *realisation*, not of position: pooling it
over windows leaves a damping map (median 0.873, 18% of cells above 1) which crashes
`tke` for a +0.09 `rel_l2` gain. **An oracle that knows the target caps the whole
per-cell fluctuation-gain class at +0.51**, and every predictable version measured is
−0.7 to −0.85. That is a ceiling on the intervention class rather than two nulls on two
instruments, and it closes the class.

**The one surviving proposal was built and it fails its pre-registered falsifier
(2026-09-02).** The gain closed above was L2-optimal and therefore energy-*reducing*;
the survivor was **energy-conserving** — relocate fluctuation across cells at fixed
window total. Its oracle is much better posed: the target's exact spatial TKE shape at
fixed total energy cuts tke error **50.1% relative for +0.00020 of `rel_l2`**, a
ceiling near +1.45. Built as `src/realistix/models/tke_shape.py` +
`tools/train_tke_shape.py`, four mimic folds, everything chosen leave-one-fold-out,
831 windows:

| | Δrel_l2 | Δtke | tke cut | Δmvpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| analytic member-map control (free) | +0.000541 | −0.013110 | **2.75%** | — |
| **learned gate** | +0.000503 | −0.009855 | **2.07%** | 7e−10 |

Against the criteria fixed in advance: tke cut ≥2.5% on 4/4 — **fail** (2.07% pooled,
only mim1 clears); `rel_l2` neutral on ≥3/4 with pooled loss <0.0002 — **fail** (0/4
neutral, +0.000503); beat the free control — **fail**. The construction checks all
pass: `mvpe` residue 7e−10, energy conserved to 5e−16, zero-gain identity reproduces
the baseline exactly, 0.0123 ms/sample. Priced: `+0.291·(2.07/10) − 0.346·(0.698/10) =
**+0.036**`, against a +0.06 bar; the free control prices to +0.054–0.059 and is also
under it.

**The decisive detail is that the learned gate loses to the zero-training control.**
The fitted map adds nothing over the member-derived one, which is the fourth
independent time a *fitted* spatial map has selected ≈ zero on these folds. The shape
headroom is real (50.1% at the oracle) and the legal signals do not reach it.
**Close the trainable TKE axis.**

**Also closed by round two:** fixed regional calibration (best 2×4 map cuts tke error

## Before anything else: the bar, and why most of this page is below it

`tools/predict_score.py` (mimic folds) resolves ~0.02-0.03 and under-reports the
board by an amount that is **not a constant**: 1.66, 0.52, 3.29 and 1.74 across the
four clean paired observations (2026-08-21), with `tke` changing sign. What does
transfer is `rel_l2`, at 0.68-1.14 wherever it moved enough to measure — so screen on
`rel_l2` and read the sign of the rest.

The folds also score *single models* while the board scores three, and that matters
more than the spread does: measured 2026-08-21 (`tools/fold_ensemble.py`),
`--mults 1,2` reads +0.005 per member and **−0.053 as an ensemble** against a board
of −0.006, so its sign is only right at ensemble level. **Score the ensemble for
anything touching capacity or member diversity.** It is not a calibration — the
transfer spread widens from 3x to 11x — and it does not rescue distillation
(+0.035 → +0.020 against a board of −0.146). **A candidate needs a predicted accuracy
delta above +0.06 to be worth a submission** (the bar reads +0.10 in `ACC_NO_SPS`
units, which under-price accuracy by 1.5-2.2x — `HANDOFF.md` §4), and its realised
size is not predictable. Nothing measured in the 08-19/21 session clears
it. Screen first; ship one variable; record the board result the same day.

Note also that **every number on this page written before 2026-08-20 is suspect in
magnitude**: fold results carried a constant-band SPS proxy that inflated them 3-4x,
and the angle folds systematically flatter capacity reductions. Re-screening the
archive through the mimic folds is the highest value-per-GPU-hour item available —
and it buys two things, not one. It corrects our own conclusions, *and* it is the
only source of paired (local, board) observations that does not cost a submission.
Until 2026-08-21 exactly one such observation was usable for a magnitude and none was
held out, which is the real reason no board score can be projected.
`tools/rescreen_archive.py` inventories what is left and what cannot be paired at
all; `HANDOFF.md` §9 item 1 has the detail.

## Read this first

**The configuration space is exhausted, and 2026-08-18 makes it three sweeps.**
(`HANDOFF.md` §9 says three as well; it said four until 2026-08-21.)
Width, depth, blocks, learning rate, EMA and schedule length have each been swept
twice — once under the original objective and again after the loss was corrected —
and are flat both times. The third pass (`tools/optuna_search.py`, TPE, **111**
trials across two seeded studies over anchor/optimiser/loss, correct loss scaling,
scored at a fixed step rather than the best one) is flat as well, and the way it is
flat is worth stating because it is the discipline that made the answer readable:

* **Do not read the maximum.** Across the 48 trials with a non-broken optimiser the
  null is mean −0.058, sd 0.110, so the expected maximum of a pure-noise draw of
  this size is `mu + sd*sqrt(2 ln n)` = **+0.249**. The best of all 111 trials was
  **+0.064** — *below* what chance alone produces. A screen's top row is a draw from the tail,
  not a finding, and the number that says so takes one line to compute.
* **Read the main effects instead**, which pool every trial rather than one:
  `anchor last` −0.057 ± 0.037 (n=10), `wmean` −0.092 ± 0.048 (n=9), `blend`
  −0.048 ± 0.018 (n=29), `out_gain` off −0.042 ± 0.015 (n=33) versus on −0.093 ±
  0.039 (n=15). Every cell negative or zero. `blend` matches `last`, reproducing
  the bf16 7-fold confirmation's −0.004 ± 0.011 on a different precision, a
  different sampler and 29 independent draws.
* **`--out-gain` is closed by the same analysis.** It appeared in four of the top
  eight rows and is negative in the pooled estimate — which is exactly what
  selecting on a maximum does to an untested variable.
* **And the fixed-step scorer earned its keep.** Over the 111 trials the
  oracle-step selection `kfold.py` uses inflates the paired delta by **+0.033 and
  +0.057** on average across the two studies — against the 0.006 it costs on four
  hand-designed variants. Randomly sampled configurations vary in eval-to-eval
  stability in a way that variants of a settled recipe do not, so the bias does not
  pair away. Reading this search through `max`-over-evals would have produced three
  to six hundredths of signal from nothing, on top of the tail-draw problem above.

And the earlier sweeps say the same. Ensemble size, seed diversity, config diversity,
architectural diversity and regime bagging are flat. The loss weights have been
searched jointly: 41 configurations over eight dimensions, and the current recipe
ranks 2nd of 39 screened, with the best draw ahead by less than the selection bias.

**Inference time looked like the exception and was not.** This file used to claim
inference had no accessible headroom because the model was ~14% of container wall
time — a number that was never measured. Re-auditing it properly (`HANDOFF.md` §4)
found real local headroom: 3.90 -> 1.15 ms/sample, accuracy untouched. The board
then made it **slower** (`time_score` 90.379 -> 90.177, 2.8x its noise) and it was
reverted. So the original conclusion was right for entirely the wrong reason, and
the new fact is sharper than either: **local inference timing does not predict this
metric, even in sign.** The one rule that survives every submission that touched
runtime is that removing real work pays and re-timing work does not.

Further knob-turning is not the constraint. Anything from here is a different
model, and each item below is a build of a day or more with an uncertain payoff.

**Calibrate against the field.** Fifty teams sit within about a point of each other.
Predictability decays hard in this flow — coherence 0.16 at high wavenumber and long
lead time — so much of the residual `rel_l2` error is irreducible, and fifty
independent efforts converging that tightly says more about the problem than about
the teams. Treat +0.15 as a good outcome for any single item below and 0 as the
median one.

**And then note the arithmetic that follows from it.** As of 2026-08-28 we are 3rd at
81.597 and the margin to the #10 cutoff is **~0.48** (`HANDOFF.md`, *Where things
stand*); it was 0.121 when this page was written, and ten submissions in ten days is
what moved it. Whether that margin holds depends on a #10 threshold whose rate this
project has never measured well: an early-August reading of ~0.081/day extrapolates
to +2.4 over the remaining 30 days and would sink everything on this page, while a
saturating field needs nothing. **The honest statement is that the required number is
not known and the range is wide.** Either way the plan is the same: a *portfolio* —
several items attempted, each banked with a submission the day it lands — because the
cheap items are the ones that finish. Submitting is free: the leaderboard keeps our
best, so a failed experiment costs a day's slot and nothing else.

(An earlier version of this paragraph put the margin at 0.416 and cited `HANDOFF.md`
§0 for it, which said 0.121, and derived "+0.5 to +1.0" from a rate implying +3.0.
Re-read the source when you cite it, and re-read the date too.)

**Prioritise by elasticity, not by weight** (`HANDOFF.md` §4). Per 10% relative
reduction in the underlying *error*: rel_l2 +0.369, **TKE +0.305**, MVPE +0.067. TKE
is worth 83% of rel_l2 at the margin — not the 21% its weight implies — and its error
is five times larger, so the percentage is much easier to find. (This used to add
"SPS +1.086", which is 10% of the *score* rather than of an error and is not
comparable to the other three; `HANDOFF.md` §4 now says why.)

## The modelling ideas, and how each one closed

Ranked, when the list was written, by whether a mechanism stood behind it. Five of
the six have since been built and measured; item 4 is the only one never run, and it
is pre-refuted by item 5. Kept as the priced record.

1. ~~**Per-cell TKE fluctuation restoration.**~~ **Built and measured 2026-08-16;
   dead** (`tools/tke_restore.py`, `HANDOFF.md` §7). Weighted +0.002 / +0.006 at the
   dose that matches the members exactly, then −0.014 and −0.042 as the dose rises,
   on both honest splits. The premise held up — averaging really does cancel
   fluctuation, by 7.6% of TKE at the median cell and 164% at p95 — and `tke` rose
   monotonically with the correction. It still lost, because `rel_l2` and SPS
   coverage give back more than TKE returns at every dose. **The +0.11 this entry
   originally claimed was wrong**: it rested on 884969 → 886080, a pair that also
   changed the head width from 96 to 48, so the +0.635 of `tke` was never
   attributable to the member count alone. Check the confound before pricing the
   idea, not after building it.

2. ~~**Horizon-aware or autoregressive rollout.**~~ **Built and measured
   2026-08-22; dead both ways.** The training-time version (`--w-unroll`: a
   second autoregressive application with gradients through both) is monotone
   negative — −0.039/−0.074/−0.114 at doses 0.25/0.5/1.0, 0/4 folds each — and
   the slow band it was meant to sharpen *worsens* (err/tgt 0.474 → 0.510):
   training through its own damped output teaches the model to operate on
   smooth inputs. `HANDOFF.md`'s 2026-08-22 block has the numbers, next to the
   low-frequency-loss result that closes the same band from the other side.

3. ~~**Warping parameterisation.**~~ **Built and measured 2026-08-18; dead**
   (`--warp`, `tools/warp_report.py`, `HANDOFF.md` §7). The premise was sound — the
   flow is advection-dominated, and predicting a *displacement field* builds
   transport in rather than asking the network to learn it. It loses on every
   anchor: `last+warp` **−0.190** (0/6), `blend+warp` **−0.200** (0/6),
   `wmean+warp` −0.030 (1/6). The *interaction* is the finding: `wmean` alone is
   −0.017, so transport costs nothing on a smooth anchor and 0.19 on a sharp one.
   The displacement head does not learn transport, it corrupts the anchor, and it
   costs most where the anchor was worth most.

   This was the last physical prior available — divergence-free is ruled out by
   measurement (`|div|/|grad|` ≈ 1.1 in *both* modalities, since the data is a 2D
   slice of a 3D flow) and pressure both by argument (a nonlocal functional of the
   velocity, carrying no independent information) and, since 2026-08-26, by
   measurement: `--aux-p` at 0.05 / 0.2 reads +0.007 / +0.003 acc-weighted over four
   mimic folds, a tenth of the bar with no dose response. With it closed, there is no
   remaining physics-motivated parameterisation on this page.

4. **Test-time augmentation.** Average predictions over small input perturbations.
   This is *variance reduction* — the opposite of the fluctuation injection that
   measured −0.37 to −9.8, which added randomness to one prediction where this
   averages it away. Never run directly, and cheap.

   **But it is largely pre-refuted, and this entry is kept only so nobody
   rediscovers it as new.** Checkpoint prediction-averaging (item 5) is the same
   mechanism — average more predictions — and it measured `rel_l2` flat, `tke`
   −0.23, net −0.03 on both honest splits, because averaging is exactly what spends
   fluctuation energy. It also costs inference time at 0.095/point (the "+0.147 track
   ceiling" this line cited was retracted 2026-09-03, `FINDINGS.md` §9c; the cost of
   extra forward passes on a launch-bound container is per pass, and higher than priced). Expected value is at best zero and probably negative; see
   "Priced and rejected without building" below. Do not spend a day on it.

5. ~~**Prediction-averaging across checkpoints.**~~ **Measured 2026-08-16 and
   dead** (`tools/swa_eval.py --modes pred`, `HANDOFF.md` §7): weighted −0.035 on
   `val_aoa` and −0.026 on `test_aoa`, the two splits agreeing. It fails the same way
   weight averaging did, and the *how* is the useful part — `rel_l2` moved by 0.01
   while `tke` fell 80.03 → 79.80, so tripling the averaging bought no accuracy and
   spent fluctuation energy. That is item 1's mechanism measured directly, on a
   controlled A/B. Re-check free on `runs/px2_s*` when it lands, since this ran on
   the pre-correction snapshots, but do not expect it to turn.

## The measurement apparatus is now the binding constraint

Stated plainly because it changes what is worth doing. `val_aoa` is 175 windows from
9 trajectories and `val_re` is 159 from 8 — and windows inside a trajectory are
strongly correlated, so the **effective sample size is the number of held-out
regimes, 8-9, not the window count**. Error bars quoted per window, or per seed with
the split held fixed, do not capture the dominant term.

**Corrected 2026-08-17 by the k-fold baseline, which measured this instead of
arguing it.** The claim above — "we cannot distinguish a real +0.1 from noise" —
was wrong, and wrong in an instructive way. Across 7 folds at 2 seeds the
**within-fold seed SD of a run is 0.025**, so a paired A/B on fixed folds resolves
about **0.02-0.03**, not 0.10. Two different things were being conflated:

* **Precision** — measuring a difference between two configurations on a fixed
  holdout — was always good, because the comparison is paired and the fold cancels.
* **Generalisation** — whether a difference found on one holdout survives another —
  is the real problem, and it is large: the fold means span **73.40 to 75.85**, an
  0.83 SD. `val_aoa` (AoA=20) is the *hardest* fold, so every headline number in
  this project is pessimistic in level, as §5 of `HANDOFF.md` suspected.
* The 0.10 swing measured yesterday came from a *fitted* post-hoc parameter
  overfitting 175 windows — a third failure mode, and the reason to distrust
  anything tuned on a split rather than trained and then evaluated on one.

So the historical "0.14 noise floor" from `val_aoa` vs `test_aoa` disagreement was
never measurement noise. It was fold-to-fold variation, i.e. exactly the quantity
k-fold exists to average over.

Two consequences, and they outrank every modelling item above:

**The k-fold rig has one known blind spot: capacity.** Every fold holds out a whole
angle and a Reynolds pair, so it trains on ~55-58 trajectories against the shipped
model's 81 — about 30% less data. A change that trades capacity for regularisation
therefore flatters itself in the folds, and `--mults 1,2` is the demonstration:
**+0.062 on 7/7 folds, −0.013 on the board** (891698), with the loss concentrated in
`tke`, the subscore most sensitive to model capacity. Trust the rig for changes that
do not interact with training-set size — losses, schedules, conditioning, post-hoc
corrections — and treat any width/depth/level change it likes as unproven until the
board says so. The fix, if this matters again, is a fold design that holds out fewer
regimes per fold at the cost of more folds.

* **k-fold over regimes** — built 2026-08-16 as `tools/kfold.py`. Seven folds, each
  holding out one whole angle *and* one disjoint Reynolds pair, scored 50/50 across
  the two shift types (a flat mean would make it an angle benchmark, since the angle
  side carries four times the trajectories). Candidates are compared **paired on the
  fold**, so the large fold-to-fold spread in level cancels and the uncertainty comes
  from the spread of the differences. About 3 GPU-hours per configuration at 2 seeds.

  ```bash
  python tools/kfold.py --tag base  --seeds 0,1          # once, the reference
  python tools/kfold.py --tag relup --seeds 0,1 --args "--w-rel 16.0"
  python tools/kfold.py --report base relup
  ```

  Nothing else on this page is worth running until the `base` reference exists.
* **Cluster the uncertainty at the trajectory level.** Some of the 3.5-SE claims in
  `HANDOFF.md` were computed across seeds at a fixed split, which holds the largest
  source of variance constant. They are not necessarily wrong, but they are not the
  interval they are being read as.

## Diagnostics worth more than another model

- **How far is the information limit?** Built 2026-08-16 as `tools/info_limit.py`
  and **running**. A ladder of cheating models, each handed part of its own target as
  extra input channels — the time-mean, then the spatial content below k = 2, 4, 8 —
  bounds how much of the residual `rel_l2` error is reachable at all. The gap between
  our 0.1044 and the cheated model's error is an *upper* bound on the headroom, and
  only a model that predicts the cheated component perfectly could collect it. If
  `k4` lands near 0.10, the forecaster is done and every remaining item above is
  worth roughly nothing; if it lands near 0.085, there is +0.5 in play and the k-fold
  apparatus is what would let us chase it safely. Either answer redirects the
  remaining weeks, which is why it goes first.

  The cheating models must never ship, so this is deliberately a standalone script

## On diffusion / generative forecasting — priced 2026-08-16, do not build

Proposed as a route to TKE and SPS. Three independent reasons it does not pay here,
any one of which is sufficient:

1. **It has already been tried in four forms**, and the most informative one is the
   conditional flow over the full residual: its spread correlated with the realised
   error at **0.572 against seed spread's 0.511** — a *better* uncertainty signal —
   and feeding it to the head moved SPS by **−0.14** (`FINDINGS.md` §5). Also on the
   list: flow ensemble for SPS (break-even, and assessed when `time_score`'s weight
   was believed to be 0 — at 0.099 it is now negative), k-split flow (79.20 against
   79.57), autoencoder + latent flow (reconstruction TKE floor too costly).

2. **The inference budget forbids it — by less than this entry used to say.**
   `time_score = 100/(1+sqrt(t/0.72896))` and the weight is 0.095. The table below
   was originally computed by scaling the *whole* 8.261 ms/sample with the number of
   forward passes, which submission 893273 refuted: removing two of three members
   saved 1.693 ms, so a pass is **0.85 ms** and **5.72 ms is fixed overhead** that no
   sampler pays twice. (2026-09-03: 2.27 of those 5.72 ms were setup, since removed —
   912717 — and the rest is launch latency on small calls, `FINDINGS.md` §9c; a pass
   at that operating point costs its launches, so this table under-prices samplers.)
   Corrected:

   | forward passes | ms/sample | `time_score` | Δ final | (as first priced) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 3 (now) | 8.26 | 90.38 | — | — |
   | 8 (8-step solve, one sample) | 12.5 | 88.42 | **−0.19** | −0.515 |
   | 64 (8 steps × 8 samples) | 59.9 | 77.72 | **−1.26** | −2.315 |
   | 256 (32 steps × 8 samples) | 222.5 | 64.41 | **−2.58** | −3.964 |

   So the cheapest usable sampler must find about **+1.8% relative SPS** before it
   scores a single point, and the 8-sample version about **+12%** — roughly a third
   of the entire distance from our head to the *oracle*. That is 2-3x less
   forbidding than this file claimed, and it is still forbidding: reasons 1 and 3
   stand untouched, and nothing on this page is worth 12%.

3. **TKE is phase-blind, which removes the advantage diffusion actually has.** The
   metric is per-cell temporal variance compared as a relative L2 over the field —
   it does not care *when* the fluctuation happens, only how much of it sits in each
   cell. So a physically plausible sample scores exactly as a per-cell amplitude
   rescale does, and any amplitude field can already be set exactly and for free by
   a scalar multiplier. That was measured today (item 1): TKE rises monotonically,
   `rel_l2` and SPS coverage give back more at every dose, on both honest splits.
   A sampler lands on the same frontier at 20-80x the cost.

The one variant not refuted is using a generative model to build a *better
conditional mean* — but that is the task diffusion is worst at, and averaging enough
samples to recover the mean is exactly what the middle column above prices.

**On flips — checked properly 2026-08-19, when the FAQ made augmentation legal.**
The organisers' FAQ of that date permits "anything that takes a released sample and
produces a variant of it on the spot during training", so mirroring is allowed by
the rules and had to be priced rather than waved away. The old one-line reason here
— "the sign of the angle of attack changes the physics" — argues against
*test-time* mirroring, where a prediction would be averaged with its mirror, and
says nothing about training time, where the mirrored input ships with its mirrored
target and the pair is a valid sample of some configuration.

**The section is the reason, and it is decisive before any measurement.** The foil
is a **NACA4418**: 4% camber at 40% chord. A mirrored NACA4418 is a NACA(−4)418,
which is a *different aerofoil*, so u ↦ u, v ↦ −v does not map this flow to any
flow of this experiment at any angle. Camber also means there is no zero-lift
geometric incidence: at AoA = 0 the foil already turns the flow, so even the
zero-angle case has no symmetry to exploit.

(Do not re-derive this from the git history without reading the dates. The chord and
section were pinned down four times; `5471978` says NACA0025 and is *superseded* by
`9c9a388`, "the datasets are not the same: NACA4418 stands, geometry measured from
this release". `git log` prints newest first, and reading it the other way makes the
retracted answer look like the current one.)

**The data agrees, which is the useful part.** Mirror the 20-frame time-mean about
row axis *a* and sweep *a* over the whole grid in half-cell steps:

| AoA | best axis | corr(u, Mu) | corr(v, −Mv) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | row 16.0 | **+0.154** | +0.354 |
| 5 | row 16.0 | +0.234 | +0.310 |
| 20 | row 15.5 | +0.719 | +0.621 |

0.154 against an expected 1.0 at the one angle where a *symmetric* section would
have to be exact. And the mean profile at AoA = 0 shows the signature directly: the
row-mean of u is flat at 0.199-0.200 above the foil and only recovers to 0.157 by
the bottom of the window, i.e. a wake deflected downward at zero geometric
incidence, which is what camber does and what a symmetric section could not.

So mirroring is legal, cheap, and wrong. The measurement is recorded because the
symmetric-section premise is an easy one to arrive at independently.

## The measurement lesson worth carrying into the modelling

The chord of the airfoil was pinned down four times, wrongly, and the most convincing
wrong answer is the one to remember. Inverting `U * c / nu` against the *measured*
Reynolds number in each file gives a chord of 20.09 cells with **2.2% spread over 81
trajectories, `corr(Re, U) = 0.9992`, and per-angle agreement to 1.5%** — and it is
wrong by **15%** against the 17.53 cells the solid footprint gives, because it needs the
organisers' reference velocity and the water temperature and neither is published.

That is the same failure as the `--mults 1,2` submission: **+0.062 on 7/7 k-fold folds,
−0.013 on the board.** Internal consistency measures how cleanly a quantity varies, not
whether the constant relating it to the thing you care about is the one you assumed. Both
times the tight spread was read as accuracy. Before trusting the next tight local number,
ask which unmeasured constant stands between it and the board.

### Amended twice: what the fp16 pool could not measure was a missing loss scaler

Recorded because the mistake is more useful than the plan was.

Eight runs diverged overnight and **every one of them carried `--warp` on an
rtx2080ti**; nothing else failed at all. The obvious reading -- warping is
unstable -- is wrong. The identical configuration, fold and seed trains to
completion in bf16 on an a100 (`runs/warpctl_bf16`, `val_aoa` 76.89, rel-L2 error
0.114, TKE 0.53: entirely healthy). So it is the precision, and the earlier
measurement that fp16 is worth a harmless **+0.063 ± 0.011** on this fold set was
answering a narrower question than it appeared to: it says fp16 is safe *for the
shipped recipe*, and says nothing about the configurations a search invents.

**And then the cause turned out to be a bug of ours.** `train_mixed.py` constructed a
`GradScaler`, printed `amp: float16 + GradScaler` into every log, and left the
training step calling `loss.backward()` and `opt.step()` directly. Every fp16 run
trained with **no loss scaling at all**. What exposed it was two divergences in
configurations carrying no `--warp` -- both `--batch 64` with a high `w_rel`, the
unscaled-gradient signature -- and the retest settles it: the exact configuration
that diverged now trains cleanly with the scaler live, rel-L2 error falling
0.175 -> 0.148 -> 0.126 through step 3000.

This is `FINDINGS.md` §9 recommitted at the other end of the pipeline. There, the
state dict was cast to fp16 and `load_state_dict` discarded it while every comment
claimed half precision. Here, a scaler was constructed, announced, and never called.
A component created and silently not applied, twice, in the same repository, within
a week. **When a log line asserts that a mechanism is active, check the call site,
not the constructor.**

A mechanism *was* proposed for why the failures clustered on `--warp`, and it should
be read as the cautionary part rather than the explanation. `grid_sample`'s gradient
with respect to displacement is proportional to the spatial gradient of the sampled
field, so a transport head reading a smooth anchor is genuinely ill-conditioned --
the aperture problem, in fluid terms. That is true, and it is not what happened. It
fitted the evidence available at the time (`wmean + warp` diverging 3/3 while nothing
else did) and it survived exactly as long as it took for a warp-free configuration to
fail. A plausible physical story is the easiest thing in the world to attach to a
bug.

**The damage was to the search, not to the runs.** A diverged trial is pruned, so
the sampler learns that a region is bad when it is only *unmeasured*, and the
region it learns to avoid is the one the study was built to test. Pruning
penalises fragility, and fragility is not badness.

Moving the axis to bf16 was therefore the right action for the wrong reason, and it
is left in place: the bf16 arm never touched the broken path, so it is the one part
of the night that needs no re-reading, and it has already answered the warp question
decisively. The fp16 studies were restarted from scratch with scaling live --
baselines included, since pairing scaled trials against unscaled baselines is the
contamination bug in a different costume.

One measurement needed re-taking, and was redone on 2026-08-19. The **+0.063 ±
0.011** fp16-versus-bf16 offset was measured on unscaled fp16 runs, so it described
a path that no longer exists. With the scaler live, over four Re folds at matched
seeds, it is **−0.005 ± 0.007** — i.e. nothing, and the two precisions are
interchangeable for this recipe. That is what unlocked the rtx2080ti and titanxp
pools for screening. Quote the new number, never the old one.

The general lesson is the one this repository keeps relearning in new clothes:
**a measurement apparatus that silently drops the cases it cannot handle reports a
biased answer, not a missing one.** It applied to `kfold.py`'s oracle step, to
averaging the folds that survived, and now to a GPU pool's exponent range.
